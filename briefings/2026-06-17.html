Weekly Intelligence Briefing

11–17 June 2026 — signals mapped to the Complexity Transition Framework.

17 June 2026 Internal — workingcomplexity Reading time 14 min

This week's signals

Methodological landscape

1. NICE publishes final draft guidance on donanemab and lecanemab — fourth rejection, including after appeal-mandated reconsideration of carer utilities

Domain 4: Coverage & Reimbursement · Handle With Care Domain 1: Evidence Standards · Handle With Care

Following the 10 June committee meeting — mandated by an appeal panel finding that NICE had not adequately assessed carer utilities and unpaid care costs — NICE has published its final draft guidance on donanemab and lecanemab, again recommending against routine NHS use. The committee concluded that the benefits of both drugs "remain too small to justify the additional costs to the NHS." This follows a January 2026 appeal upheld on grounds that carer utility assessment was unreasonable, a new March 2026 consultation, a revised EAG analysis specifically incorporating caregiver utility values and unpaid care costs, and a consultation closed 28 April. Manufacturers retain the right to appeal this final draft guidance — a fourth appeal in this appraisal's history.

Why it matters to workingcomplexity

The last three briefings tracked this as the test of whether carer utility recalibration would change NICE's conclusion. The result is a clear empirical answer: it did not. The committee reviewed improved carer burden data as the appeal panel required and still found the cost-effectiveness estimate substantially above the acceptable threshold. The problem is structural, not parametric: a QALY-based framework captures additional carer utility as a larger QALY gain for a fixed drug cost, improving the ratio but not closing the gap. This is the evidential foundation for a publishable piece this week: "Why fixing the QALY calculation does not fix the QALY problem."

2. NICE cost-effectiveness threshold raised from April 2026 (£25k–£35k per QALY) — first change since NICE's inception, tested immediately by the Alzheimer's final draft guidance

Domain 4: Coverage & Reimbursement · Handle With Care

From April 2026, NICE's cost-effectiveness thresholds have been raised from £20,000–£30,000 to £25,000–£35,000 per QALY — the first change since NICE was established in 1999, following a DHSC consultation announced in December 2025. NICE estimates the change will result in three to five additional medicines recommended per year. Separately, NICE is finalising adoption of the new EQ-5D-5L UK value set (consultation closed 13 May 2026); adoption will apply only to appraisals started after the updated manuals are published. These two changes were the main structural tools available to NICE to address criticism that its QALY framework systematically undervalues disease burden in conditions like Alzheimer's.

Why it matters to workingcomplexity

Both tools have now been deployed — threshold raised, carer burden recalibrated — and neither tipped the Alzheimer's assessment. This is a compound event: the most visible framework upgrades NICE has made in a generation were tested by a live appraisal in the same week, and the outcome was unchanged. The argument is now empirically grounded rather than theoretical: "The framework has been updated. The updates are real. The gap is structural, not parametric." This shifts the argument from "NICE should update its methods" to "NICE has updated its methods and it is not enough."

3. CHMP May 2026 approves nerandomilast for IPF and progressive pulmonary fibrosis — evidence package satisfies standards for a hard-to-characterise population

Domain 2: Approval & Licensing · Build Domain 1: Evidence Standards · Handle With Care → Build

At its 18–21 May 2026 meeting, the EMA's CHMP recommended Jascayd (nerandomilast) for both idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and progressive pulmonary fibrosis — a broader indication than the FDA approval (IPF only, October 2025). The FIBRONEER-ILD trial supporting the PPF indication enrolled a heterogeneous population of ILD patients with progressive fibrosis of different aetiology, a population difficult to define diagnostically. Clinical Trial Vanguard's analysis characterised the CHMP session as signalling "a structural shift in European drug development," noting the committee's "appetite for new mechanistic evidence, not just incremental data stacked on an existing dossier."

Why it matters to workingcomplexity

The NICE committee meets on 15 July — now less than four weeks away — to assess nerandomilast against a narrower scope than the EMA approved. The EMA accepted an evidentiary package for a hard-to-characterise PPF population; NICE will assess whether its economic model can accommodate a drug whose PDE4B inhibition mechanism does not map cleanly to existing treatment pathway models. The CHMP acceptance is now a direct comparator for the NICE assessment: the regulatory body most comparable to NICE in standards accepted the evidence as adequate for a harder population. The "seven mechanisms" brief should lead with this comparison.

Competitive landscape

4. New Health Secretary James Murray at ConfedExpo — "We cannot choose between performance and reform": technology and AI framed as the route to system sustainability

Domain 7: Commissioning & Contracting · Handle With Care Domain 8: Stakeholder Engagement · Handle With Care

James Murray — appointed Health Secretary approximately four weeks before ConfedExpo — gave the conference keynote on 11 June 2026, rejecting the proposition that the NHS must choose between day-to-day performance and long-term structural reform. Murray positioned technology adoption, AI integration, and reduction of bureaucratic overhead as essential for system sustainability, citing progress in waiting list reduction and primary care access. The NHS Confederation published a response welcoming the reform direction while flagging the need for "clarity on accountability structures as the Health Bill enters scrutiny."

Why it matters to workingcomplexity

A new Health Secretary at the start of committee stage scrutiny of a major NHS reorganisation Bill is a meaningful configuration. Murray's "performance and reform are not alternatives" framing positions technology-led transformation as the answer to system sustainability — a complicated-systems answer applied to what the evidence base identifies as a complex-systems problem. The NHS Confederation's emphasis on accountability structures echoes the King's Fund's five-tests framework. This framing window is measured in weeks, not months, and is currently open.

5. NHS ConfedExpo post-conference consensus — "transform its way out, not manage its way through": AI shifts from pilots to clinical responsibility; data governance named as critical gatekeeper

Domain 7: Commissioning & Contracting · Handle With Care Domain 3: Performance Measurement · Handle With Care

Post-conference reflections characterised ConfedExpo 2026 as marking a "decisive shift from abstract digital strategies to the pragmatic delivery of the NHS 10-Year Health Plan." Themes: the transition from fragmented local trusts to a unified Single Patient Record; a shift from AI pilots to "concrete clinical responsibility" for AI-enabled clinical decisions; and health equity risks from technology-led transformation not designed alongside communities. Speakers repeatedly emphasised that public trust and robust data governance are "the actual gatekeepers" for data infrastructure ambitions.

Why it matters to workingcomplexity

The shift from AI pilots to "concrete clinical responsibility" is precisely the moment where the absence of Build-zone performance measurement frameworks (Domain 3) becomes a concrete operational risk. "Concrete clinical responsibility" for AI requires a measurement framework capable of assessing what AI changes in complex, longitudinal patient outcomes — not just task completion rates. ConfedExpo has named the accountability gap without framing it theoretically. This is an entry point for workingcomplexity's Domain 3 argument that starts from the question the conference identified and works backwards to what measures would answer it.

6. HSSIB ePMA safety report (28 May 2026) — NHS trusts carrying unassigned digital safety risk; significant unwarranted variation in electronic prescribing governance

Domain 5: Safety & Surveillance · Handle With Care → Build Domain 3: Performance Measurement · Handle With Care

Prominent in post-ConfedExpo commentary, the Health Services Safety Investigations Body's investigation into Electronic Prescribing and Medicines Administration (ePMA) systems found that NHS trusts manage complex digital safety risks without adequate central support, regulatory clarity, or consistent governance. Significant unwarranted variation exists in how ePMA software is designed, procured, and regulated across acute hospitals. HSSIB concluded trusts are being asked to carry the responsibility of assuring ePMA software safety: "a complex and resource-intensive task — not all trusts have the capacity, capability or support they need to do it robustly."

Why it matters to workingcomplexity

The HSSIB report is a concrete instance of Build-zone capabilities (learning digital health infrastructure, adaptive safety monitoring) being developed without Build-zone accountability structures to match them. There is a notable irony for Modernisation Bill scrutiny: HSSIB — the body that just published findings arguing for more independent oversight of NHS digital safety — is the same body the Health Bill proposes to merge into the CQC, reducing independent oversight in the same week it demonstrates why independent oversight is needed.

Policy & regulatory

7. NHS Modernisation Bill: line-by-line scrutiny opens 16 June — first oral evidence heard; King's Fund "five tests" frames the complexity argument for parliament

Domain 7: Commissioning & Contracting · Handle With Care Domain 8: Stakeholder Engagement · Handle With Care

The Health Bill's Public Bill Committee began oral evidence on 16 June 2026, moving to clause-by-clause scrutiny from 18 June, with a report due by 5pm on 16 July. Written evidence submitted now receives approximately five weeks of consideration. The King's Fund published "Five tests for the NHS Modernisation Bill 2026" as scrutiny opened: (1) does centralisation of powers deliver better outcomes?; (2) are there adequate checks and balances on the expanded Secretary of State powers?; (3) does reform support rather than displace local adaptation?; (4) are accountability structures proportionate to the complexity of the change?; (5) is there credible evidence that structural reform alone will improve patient care?

Why it matters to workingcomplexity

The King's Fund's five tests are directly aligned with the analytical frame workingcomplexity has built across six weeks of briefings: complicated-systems design logic applied to a complex-systems commissioning problem. The King's Fund has formalised this argument through a parliamentary scrutiny framework without using complexity vocabulary. Written evidence can reference the five tests and extend them — specifically, the "does it support local adaptation?" test can be given the theoretical basis the King's Fund does not provide: why local adaptation is structural capability rather than bureaucratic friction.

8. Neighbourhood health contracts: Stage 1 provider contracts roll out 2026/27 — new sub-ICS accountability structures for complex-care populations

Domain 7: Commissioning & Contracting · Build Domain 6: Guideline Development · Handle With Care

The NHS 10-Year Health Plan's neighbourhood health framework enters operational implementation in 2026/27 with two new provider contracts for place-based, integrated care at sub-ICB level. Stage 1 (2026/27) covers immediate structural changes including neighbourhood health centres, MDT staffing, and expanded hours; Stage 2 (April 2027–March 2029) covers longer-term reform. The contracts create accountability for population health outcomes at neighbourhood level, including for patients with multimorbidity whose care needs do not fit disease-specific commissioning frameworks.

Why it matters to workingcomplexity

Neighbourhood health contracts are the Build-zone candidate in Domain 7 that is most operationally advanced — commissioning structures explicitly designed for complexity, developed in direct tension with a Bill that centralises commissioning powers above them. Whether these are complementary or contradictory depends on whether the contracts are given genuine decision-making autonomy or implemented as delivery vehicles for centrally-determined plans — a question the King's Fund's "adequate checks and balances" test directly addresses.

Therapeutic areas

9. IPF: three weeks to the nerandomilast NICE committee — CHMP PPF extension and raised threshold add to evidence landscape; "seven mechanisms" brief window closing

Domain 1: Evidence Standards · Build Domain 2: Approval & Licensing · Build

The NICE committee meeting for nerandomilast (ID6446) remains on 15 July 2026, with Aberdeen HERU/HSRU as EAG and expected guidance publication on 9 September. Three factors sharpen the stakes: (1) the CHMP approved nerandomilast for PPF as well as IPF — a regulatory precedent that evidence is adequate for a heterogeneous fibrotic population; (2) the cost-effectiveness threshold has been raised to £25–35k, marginally improving approval probability without changing evidentiary questions about mechanism, surrogacy, and comparator selection; (3) rentosertib's Phase 3 (same TNIK pathway) remains on track for H2 2026 initiation — precedents this committee sets about AI-discovered mechanisms will apply within 18–24 months.

Why it matters to workingcomplexity

The brief needs to be circulating before 15 July. Three factors converge this week: "NICE is more restrictive than EMA for the same drug in the same disease space" is now concrete and citable; the Alzheimer's case demonstrates the limits of QALY recalibration separately from IPF; and the rentosertib Phase 3 timing means this is a recurring category. The brief's scope should extend from "nerandomilast at NICE" to "HTA confronts AI-discovered mechanisms for the first time." Three weeks remain.

10. Rare disease: SMC June 2026 decisions — odevixibat (PFIC), sparsentan (IgAN), dupilumab extension (CRSwNP) accepted; patterns in small-N evidence navigation

Domain 4: Coverage & Reimbursement · Handle With Care → Build Domain 1: Evidence Standards · Handle With Care

The Scottish Medicines Consortium published advice on seven medicines on 8 June 2026. Odevixibat (Bylvay) was accepted for progressive familial intrahepatic cholestasis — a paediatric ultra-rare liver condition where surrogate endpoints (serum bile acid levels) and single-arm studies were accepted given population size and absence of alternatives. Sparsentan (Filspari) was accepted for IgA nephropathy using a surrogate (proteinuria/eGFR slope) whose causal relationship to kidney function preservation has been contested in some regulatory contexts. Both decisions use deliberative process to work around thin evidence.

Why it matters to workingcomplexity

The SMC decisions are working examples of HTA bodies navigating exactly the domains where the Complexity Transition Framework predicts stress. Neither decision represents a resolved methodological solution — both are deliberative workarounds around an unchanged GRADE core. This is the "patch vs. redesign" pattern at the rare disease level, directly comparable to NICE's Alzheimer's assessments: workarounds accumulate around an unchanged core method, and the ISPOR rare disease stakeholder survey (10 June briefing) confirms GRADE as the persistent default even where evidence is thinnest.

11. Severe asthma: ELBRUS post-reimbursement RWE study architecture — evidence generation without a formal feedback mechanism into coverage conditions

Domain 1: Evidence Standards · Handle With Care → Build Domain 5: Safety & Surveillance · Handle With Care

ELBRUS (NCT06566885) is a 12-month, multicenter, non-interventional post-reimbursement RWE study assessing patient-reported outcomes after tezepelumab initiation in participants with severe asthma. It is distinct from the two real-world cohorts flagged in the last two briefings (the ERJ Open Research retrospective, the PASSAGE Phase 4 study): it is specifically a post-reimbursement accountability study, designed to generate evidence in the broader populations now treated in practice. It does not, however, include a mechanism by which data feeds back into coverage conditions, label updates, or HTA reviews — a manufacturer-led PRO study running in parallel to the reimbursement system, not within it.

Why it matters to workingcomplexity

Three independent post-reimbursement real-world datasets now exist for tezepelumab in severe asthma, each generating data on populations the registration trial excluded. ELBRUS illustrates the "Handle With Care → Build" pattern in Domain 1 at its clearest: real-world evidence is being generated post-reimbursement (Build intent), but there is no pathway by which that evidence updates coverage conditions (Handle With Care reality). Severe asthma is now the best-documented example of this gap — RWE generation is at scale and the accountability loop is visibly absent.

Reading the table

This week is structured around a single compound methodological event: NICE's Alzheimer's final draft guidance proves that the two main upgrades available to the QALY framework — a higher threshold and better-measured carer utilities — are insufficient to resolve the cost-effectiveness problem for disease-modifying therapies with high carer burden. This happens in the same week that the Bill governing all NHS commissioning enters its most scrutinisable legislative phase, with the King's Fund's five parliamentary tests formalising the complexity-vs-centralisation argument. The nerandomilast committee (15 July) and the Modernisation Bill report (16 July) both land on the same day — the fourth week of July is the next significant concentration of domain events.

Recommended actions

  1. Domain 4 · Handle With Care Domain 1 · Handle With Care
    Write the "QALY recalibration vs. QALY redesign" piece this week using the Alzheimer's final draft guidance as the anchor

    The empirical case is now complete: NICE reviewed revised carer utility values as the appeal panel required, the threshold has been raised to its highest level since NICE's establishment, and the outcome is still a recommendation against. Structure: (1) what changed — carer utilities and threshold; (2) what didn't change — the verdict; (3) why not — the gap is structural, not parametric; (4) what would need to change — a different instrument for disease-modifying therapies in conditions with high carer burden. Publish while the final draft guidance is live and the appeal window is open.
